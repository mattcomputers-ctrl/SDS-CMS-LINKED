# SDS data sources — post-implementation audit (2026-10-09)

Where every field on a generated SDS comes from after the 42 audit items were implemented. The owner-facing summary is the PDF *SDS Data Source Map* (same content, laid out like an SDS). Findings for review are in [sds-data-sources-findings.md](sds-data-sources-findings.md). Nothing was changed by this audit.

## Summary

Every safety data sheet is now built from your data each time it is generated. The formula and each raw material's records produce the hazard classification. That classification picks the wording for most sections, from first aid through disposal and transport. Raw-material properties supply the physical data, your regulatory lists supply Section 15, and Admin Settings, product families and manufacturer records supply the supplier block, use text and disclaimer. The 16-section layout, the labels and the regulatory phrases are fixed text, translated into English, Spanish, French and German. Where an operator has typed per-product text for a language, that text prints instead of the automatic text.

Before this update, only about a third of the sheet came from data. Most paragraphs (first aid, firefighting, spills, handling, stability, toxicology, ecology, disposal, regulatory status) were the same canned sentences on every product. Several of them were English on every language sheet. Those paragraphs now change with each product's hazards, physical state and ingredients. Concentrations print as prescribed ranges instead of exact percentages. Transport is classified for the mixture as a whole, not copied from one ingredient. The editor saves only text an operator actually types, so automatic text is no longer frozen when a product is saved.

**Key points**

- A blank emergency phone number blocks publishing. Standard sheets read it from Admin Settings only. Private-label sheets read only the manufacturer's own number.
- A Section 14 transport result of 'Not determined' (no flash point and no hazard data) blocks publishing until the data is filled in or a UN number or hazard class is entered as an override.
- Raw-material data now drives much of the sheet: flash point, boiling point, physical state, odor, appearance, solubility, specific gravity and VOC feed Sections 5, 6, 8, 9, 13 and 14, so gaps show up as 'Not determined'.
- Keep the regulatory lists current (TSCA, SARA 313, HAP, SNUR, Prop 65, carcinogens, RCRA waste codes). They decide what Sections 11 to 15 say, and a TSCA gap prints 'not verified' and raises an operator warning.
- Maintain product-family use text and the disclaimer in each language, and manufacturer records for private label. Otherwise ES/FR/DE sheets fall back to generic or English text.
- Regulatory wording stays fixed: GHS hazard and precautionary statements, the 0.1% disclosure cut-off, the prescribed concentration ranges, 49 CFR shipping names, Prop 65 safe-harbor wording and the CFR/ANSI/NFPA citations.
- Per-product edits now hold only text someone typed and can be reset to automatic. Edits saved under the old editor may still carry outdated wording and should be reviewed.
